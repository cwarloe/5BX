## Timestamp table

The first 5 minutes 35 seconds are an introductory argument for fitness (office work, emergencies, aircrew, firefighters). The five exercises are then demonstrated, first as the Chart 2 set, then as selected later-chart variations and common corrections. After 10:41 the narrator walks through the printed charts and the way repetition counts increase.

## What the film settles

These points are stated by the narrator and visible in the footage.

- In Charts 1 to 3, running on the spot lifts the feet only about 4 inches off the floor.
- In Charts 4 to 6, the knees are raised waist high.
- The Chart 2 jump is a stride jump with the arms raising sideways.
- The Chart 4 jump is a full squat with a straight back, and the feet alternate on each jump.
- The Chart 3 push-up goes chin, then forehead, then arms straight, with the back kept straight.
- In Charts 4 to 6, Ex1 is a full circle: the hands sweep from the floor-touch and the body bends backward as far as possible on the circle.
- The Chart 2 sit-up comes fully upright, and the back is kept straight throughout.
- The Chart 4 sit-up keeps the arms straight and close against the ears, and carries them in a wide arc to touch the toes.
- Chart 2, Ex3 is a prone lift of the head, chest, and both legs together. It is not an alternate leg raise.

## Not shown from the side

A true side view is only used in a few places. Most of the film is three-quarter front, a high three-quarter, or a head-on front.

- Ex1 is never shown from the side, neither the Chart 2 toe touch and lean-back nor the Chart 4-6 full circle.
- The Chart 2 astride jump is only three-quarter front, and mid-air frames have motion blur at 24 frames per second. 07:26.4 is the clearest still with the arms out and the feet off the floor.
- The Chart 4 squat jump and the Chart 4-6 high-knee run are only three-quarter front, so the "straight back" on the squat is a little harder to judge than it would be in profile.
- The Chart 1 knee push-up and the Chart 4 wide-hand push-up are a high three-quarter, not a side view.
- The Chart 5 clap and Chart 6 chest-slap push-ups are filmed from the front.
- The Chart 4 straight-arm sit-up is a high three-quarter, not a true side profile. The Chart 2 sit-up, by contrast, does have a clear side view from 06:23.
- The 4-inch run is a side close-up of the shins and feet, not a full-body side view.

The film also does not demonstrate every chart of every exercise. The complete set on screen is Chart 2. Ex4 is then shown from Chart 1 through Chart 6, and a few later-chart corrections are shown for Ex1, Ex2, and Ex5. Chart 1 Ex2 (the partial sit-up) and Chart 1 Ex3 (the alternate leg raise) are not shown. That last gap matters because Chart 2 Ex3 in this film is a different movement from the Chart 1 alternate leg raise.
