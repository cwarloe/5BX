# 5BX photographic pose generation — temporary limb-marker control

**Working experiment note · 2026-11-26 · assistant QA only.** This documents what we *observed* in Chart 1 still-image tests. It is not a verified source-video anatomy trace, a deterministic pose-control system, proof of correct AI video interpolation, or Charles's approval of an exercise for release.

## Why this experiment matters

The generator repeatedly drew attractive four-pose sheets in which the apparent same leg performed both alternate raises, or the scissor jump repeated one front/back leg assignment. Text-only instructions like “switch the physical foreground leg,” or recoloring a finished image's screen-left shoe, were insufficient evidence that the same *anatomical* limb survived from phase to phase. The [earlier scissor diagnostic record](../../SCISSOR_JUMP_DIAGNOSTIC_RECORD.md) describes these failures, as does the [photographic video pilot playbook](../../PHOTO_VIDEO_PILOT_PLAYBOOK.md).

In the new tests, the useful change was to add narrow, high-contrast **temporary bands to specific limbs before generating the multi-pose card** and explicitly require each band to remain attached to one hip→knee→ankle or shoulder→elbow→wrist chain. This gives both the generator and the reviewer a visible continuity constraint. Crucially, the markers are a *model-output diagnostic*, not a verified tracking tag affixed to the person in the original 1959 film or the owner's demonstration video.

## Reproducible image-stage procedure

1. **Select source authorities by role.** Use the user-approved v2 photo identity master for the *same* ordinary, soft, unmuscular 13–14-year-old face, proportions, hair, fair skin, blue tank, charcoal shorts, dark socks, and gray/yellow shoes. Use original 5BX booklet/film and applicable approved movement exemplar for *exercise mechanics*. Neither adult reference anatomy nor a previously incorrect AI pose becomes identity authority.
2. **State an observable physical ownership rule, not a screen-position label.** For the fixed camera, assign bright **ORANGE** narrowly to the physical camera-near ankle and **TEAL** to the physical camera-far ankle. For scissor jumps add matching-color tags to the respective same-side wrists. The tag must follow an identifiable pelvis→knee→shoe or shoulder→elbow→hand chain through *every* panel. Saying “the leftmost shoe is orange” is not this control: screen position changes in an actual exchange.
3. **Generate the entire ordered card together.** A four-panel contact card has one immutable camera, figure scale, background, clothing, and precise A/B/C/D pose instructions. Specify *which marked limb* moves and which stays planted at each phase. Include complete shoes and enough margins, normal short range of motion, and athletic rather than ballet feet. If the hard move needs a near-front or true side camera, choose form legibility over a universal 30° rule.
4. **For scissor, couple arms explicitly.** Leg markers alone corrected the exchanged legs but **did not** move the correct arms. Use four tags and test the alternating “X”: A/D orange **ankle aft, wrist fore**; B/C orange **ankle fore, wrist aft**; teal reverses. Require the actual shoulder and elbow to move—not just wrist colors to swap. Both front/back grounded endpoints, an honest low airborne exchange, and a return to the same starting assignment should be visible.
5. **QA the marked card before any cleaning or video.** In each tile independently trace tag→joint→hip or shoulder; verify near/far occlusion, full shoe, opposite arm/leg pairing, proper ground contact, constant face/hair/age/build, and no duplicate or detached limb. Make a four-panel matrix rather than trusting one plausible still or the text of the prompt. Keep tagged failures as **REJECT** and tagged successes as **DIAGNOSTIC**, never `APPROVED`.
6. **Remove the markers with a single-variable edit.** Feed the *successful marked card as edit target* and the untouched identity master as clothing/appearance authority. Request restoration of uniform dark socks and ordinary wristbands **only**, with strict preservation of physical chains, support points, hip/shoulder overlap, camera, environment, and panel order. Then inspect the entire image again. The image editor is generative, not guaranteed pixel-masked; significant adjacent changes can occur even under “do not change anything else.” Prefer no extra unneeded edit iterations.
7. **Preserve evidence and exact inputs.** Retain the tagged diagnostic, every clean candidate, the original complete prompts, reference filenames and ordering, dimensions, model, date, a concise QA/reject table, and any measured similarity with caveats. Link the specific artifacts in the mobile Drive review index. GitHub stores safe process/prompt docs, not a substitute mobile video gallery.
8. **Only then test motion with a single clean start anchor.** The old three-frame video workflow sometimes reversed phase order or produced ghosts. Use one high-quality start photo and explicit timed movement/recovery language where supported. Inspect the *actual video* at normal speed and sampled frames for correct limb lineage, form, identity, stationary camera and loop seam; a correct pose card does not prove a correct video.

## What is known versus still a hypothesis

- **Observed:** With these particular imagegen inputs, visually persistent ankle tags were associated with a correct-looking opposite single-leg raise, and four wrist+ankle tags with a correct-looking scissor cross-opposition. This is a promising prompt-control and review method; do not present it as deterministic or guaranteed across generators.
- **Observed:** Scissor V3 fixed leg topology but not arm switching. Adding wrist ownership in V4 fixed the *visible* arm error. Merely saying “opposite arms” was not sufficient in V3.
- **Observed:** The generated camera remained near strict side profile despite a requested 25–35° oblique view. It improved silhouette simplicity but **reduces the ability to independently prove depth and anatomical left/right** after marker removal.
- **Observed:** Cleaning Ex3 tags altered some non-target image details, despite a surgical edit prompt. An untagged result cannot inherit the diagnostic's pass unconditionally.
- **Unverified:** A generated near/far band corresponds to the actual anatomical left/right leg of the *adult* in the source video. The original demonstration's shoes are often cropped/occluded and no source physical marker was recorded. The tagged AI card validates its own visual continuity, not provenance across the source→AI boundary.
- **Unverified:** An AI video will interpolate the correct exchange, keep tags logically connected when removed, or loop naturally. The marked scissor V4 was a diagnostic still set, not a completed video motion test. Smooth video and correct form are separate gates.
- **Unverified:** Colors alone explain the improvement. The combined high-specificity anatomical language, role of prior card reference, and single unified contact sheet changed together in some tests. Controlled comparative trials would be needed to isolate each causal contribution.

**Release rule:** Mark all these as assistant-level candidates/diagnostics until Charles explicitly reviews a specific movement. A beautiful still is not a verified 5BX repetition, and an appealing video is not necessarily a mechanically correct demonstration.
