## Reusable lessons

- Use the historical 5BX source film and booklet to specify body mechanics, then preserve the approved middle-school photographic identity separately. A plausible image is not automatically correct exercise form.
- Favor complete three-/four-position cards when exploring a movement: they reduce visual age, outfit, and camera drift compared with unrelated isolated frames.
- A camera angle around 20–35 degrees often helps distinguish near/far limbs; a near-frontal view is better for some outside-foot toe-touch exercises.
- Keep realistic proportions and flexibility for a 13–14-year-old. Reject anatomically distorted pike/forehead or knee push-up frames rather than forcing unrealistic exact contact.
- Grounded jumping and natural landing mechanics should keep running-shoe soles and toes neutral; avoid ballet-style pointed toes.
- Transparent photographic PNG cutouts are feasible, but generated background removal can subtly change face/pose/framing and leave edge-color fringe. Verify alpha **and** visual fidelity before reusing in animation.
- For motion, one stable up-position anchor sometimes interpolates better than multiple misaligned frames; test complete cycles for return motion, ghosts, and leg identity.
